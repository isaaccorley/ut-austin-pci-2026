# Sources and asset attribution

Scientific figures and media come from the sources below. White-background figures use a CSS multiply blend to match the slide background; the underlying files are unmodified. The Quarto theme derives from the [CNG talk](https://github.com/isaaccorley/cng-mapping-digital-public-goods/tree/f7517e1be8466bc4a4793f29e03b322e95ccaa2f).

This November 10, 2026 course edition adapts the [UT Austin GIScience Colloquium talk](https://github.com/isaaccorley/ut-austin-giscience-2026) at `1f3066d`. Research figures, media and statistics retain their original provenance below. The cover and social graphics use the new course, date and slides URL.

- Added change clips and posters (`assets/motion/{matopiba-change,masindi-change,ili-change,kura-change,bahia-layout-change}*`): copied without modification from the [CNG talk](https://github.com/isaaccorley/cng-mapping-digital-public-goods/tree/f7517e1be8466bc4a4793f29e03b322e95ccaa2f/assets/media) at `f7517e1`. Its [capture notes](https://github.com/isaaccorley/cng-mapping-digital-public-goods/blob/f7517e1be8466bc4a4793f29e03b322e95ccaa2f/MEDIA.md) describe October 8, 2026 RGB-only Sentinel-2 comparison wipes, without field-boundary overlays. Matopiba, Ili and Kura compare 2018/2025 Q3; Masindi and West Bahia compare 2018/2025 Q1. Each preserves the camera, quarter, bands and RGB stretch across years. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data, via CDSE; original capture attribution is retained in the slides. These selected examples do not establish area totals, water use or causes of change.
- SatCLIP pipeline (`assets/papers/satclip-fig2.png`, slide `#location-encoder-intro`): unmodified [Figure 2 image](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17179v3/satclip_full_latest.png) from Klemmer et al., [SatCLIP: Global, General-Purpose Location Embeddings with Satellite Imagery](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17179v3#S2.F2), CC BY 4.0. Shows image-location pretraining and downstream use of the location encoder with labels. This is the exact figure requested for the course edition.
- AlphaEarth embedding fields (`assets/papers/alphaearth-fig1-b-e.png`, slide `#using-satellite-embeddings`): Figure 1 panels B–E from Brown et al., [AlphaEarth Foundations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22291v2#page=2), page 2. Extracted with PyMuPDF from the original PDF at 288 dpi, using the rectangle `(62, 300, 535, 666)` in PDF points. Only the selected panels are shown; labels, observations and colors are unchanged. Panel A's performance comparison and the caption are outside the crop. © 2025 Google DeepMind; paper citation retained on the slide.
- TESSERA downstream use (`assets/papers/tessera-fig10.png`, slide `#tessera-embeddings`): unmodified [Figure 10 image](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20380v2/figures/downstream_demo.png) from Feng et al., [TESSERA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20380v2#A4.F10), CC BY 4.0. Compares conventional encoder-based workflows with a small task-specific head fitted to pre-generated embeddings. Original image transparency is preserved; the slide background shows through. These paper-figure slides introduce existing methods and make no new performance claim.

- FTW labels and prediction examples: [FTW paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16252v2), CC BY 4.0. The training-loop SVG illustrates the original benchmark baseline, distinct from the four-quarter global model.
- FTW gallery: existing Sorriso, Flevoland, Mazovia and Overberg captures, 2025 Q3 Sentinel-2 imagery with published field outlines. Al-Jawf compares 2018/2025 Q3 (Saudi Arabia comparison slide); the older Masindi still pair is unused, with the course comparison using the CNG clip described above. Same camera and RGB stretch within each pair. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data, via CDSE. These examples do not support new accuracy, crop, ownership or causal claims.
- FTW pipeline figure: adapted from the CNG talk. Current stage descriptions follow the [global FTW pipeline](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/global-ftw-2e). The [second-edition preview](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/global-ftw-2e/) explicitly reports regional under-detection in 2017 and 2024.
- coarsen figure: eight FTW fixture polygons and the documented shared-edge example, 5 m simplification in local UTM for the parcels. Historical parity checks are documented in the original deck; this adaptation runs no new geometry experiment. [coarsen benchmarks](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/coarsen/tree/2685afe78d34bc6a3a640d21662df9eb4485a766/benchmarks).
- MIND figures and maps: [v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25454v2), [project page](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/mind/) and [repository figures](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/mind/tree/main/docs/assets). Original architecture, width curves, main results, Figure 5 and Figure 10 are retained. Feature maps use separate PCA fits; colors are not classes or comparable semantics.
- FTW and MIND MP4s: project-browser recordings with JPEG poster fallbacks.
- Demo poster stills (`assets/stills/ftw-2e-viewer-poster.jpg`, `assets/stills/ftw-olmoearth-poster.jpg`): headless Chrome captures at 1600 × 900 of the [Global FTW 2nd Edition preview](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/global-ftw-2e/#year=2025&q=Q3&mode=tc&pm=1&pmm=coverage) and [FTW × OlmoEarth](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/ftw-olmoearth/#10.00/42.0000/-93.6000) on October 28, 2026, saved as JPEG at quality 85. They are offline fallbacks for the live iframes only. Basemaps inside them are Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) and OpenFreeMap labels (© OpenStreetMap contributors); the apps carry their own attribution.
- FTW-2E scale figures (`assets/scale/`, October 28, 2026): generated by `scripts/figures/ftw_scale.py` from the published [raster and vector indexes](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data-2e) (`index/raster.parquet`, `index/vector.parquet`). The tile map colours each of the 7,467 2025 tiles by its published `field_frac` (share of pixels with p(field) > 0.5); the ratio map divides 2024 by 2023 `field_frac` per tile; the bar chart sums `n_parcels` per year. Inference windows in `scripts/figures/data/inference_windows.csv` are the `inference_*` rows of `scripts/experiments/scaling/data/run_timeline.csv` in global-ftw-2e at `4b38637`; the other run numbers on the compute slide (41 s per tile, 28.5k vCPU-hours, ~2,200 vCPUs, ~50 h, AWS cost) are quoted from [`docs/reports/pipeline-scaling.md`](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/global-ftw-2e/blob/main/docs/reports/pipeline-scaling.md), whose end-of-run figures are projections. Land outlines: Natural Earth 1:50m land, public domain.
- FTW model inputs (`assets/ftw-inputs/`): `scripts/figures/ftw_inputs.py` reads one 4 km window in tile `15TVG_0_0` (Marshall County, Iowa) from the 2025 Q1–Q4 quarterly mosaics mirrored on Source Cooperative (`tge-labs/sentinel-2-quarterly-cloudless-mosaics`, viewer stretch 0–3000, gamma 0.7) and from the published 2025 probability COG. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data.
- Pipeline step crops (`assets/ftw-steps/`): unmodified crops from global-ftw-2e `docs/figures/crops/` (commit `2f8e92a`), generated by `scripts/figures/make_pipeline_crops.py` there; the BoundaryVote panels replay `fbp.FTW_2E` on window W1 of tile `32ULD_0_0`, and the distribution panels show the released COG, zone file, PMTiles and parcels. See that repository's `docs/pipeline-architecture.md`.
- Evaluation schematics (`assets/eval/`): `scripts/figures/eval_figs.py`. The fold maps use the 2025 FTW-2E tile centres as example locations with illustrative random and 20° cell fold assignments; the CoordBench cell maps draw 2°, 10°, 20° and 40° cells over Natural Earth land with one illustrative held-out fold; the Chunk Penalization chart is a schematic with γ = 2. None of them is a MIND result.
- MIND storage chunks and half-sill figure (`assets/mind-chunks/`, `assets/mind-original/fig-halfsill.svg`): unmodified from the [MIND repository](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/mind/tree/main/docs/assets) `docs/assets/chunks/chunk_{00,08,24,47}.png` and `fig-halfsill.svg` (main at `eb25937`). Each chunk map uses its own PCA.
- Zoom clip (`assets/motion/ftw-zoom.mp4`, poster `ftw-zoom-poster.jpg`, October 28, 2026): 660 frames rendered one at a time with headless Chrome from a local copy of the FTW-2E viewer (global-ftw-2e `index.html` and `web/`) reading the published PMTiles, GeoParquet and Q3 2025 mosaics from Source Cooperative. Each frame waited for the map to go idle; 30 fps, 20 s move plus 2 s hold, H.264 CRF 26.5. The camera goes from lon −40, lat 22 (MapLibre zoom 2) to centre pivots near Dalhart, Texas (lon −102.66, lat 36.12, zoom 12.5). Render-only changes in the copy: UI, tile footprints and attribution hidden; PMTiles fields drawn as outlines, without fill; outlines recoloured `#8aa0d4` at 2.2 px to match the earlier stills; hexagon levels cross-faded. Basemaps: EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2025 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), OpenFreeMap labels (© OpenStreetMap contributors). Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data.
- Additional gallery stills (`assets/gallery/{kansas,japan,argentina,uzbekistan}-2e.jpg`): 1280 × 720 captures from the same patched viewer copy, Q3 2025 true-colour mosaic with 2025 outlines, matching the earlier stills. The views are Haskell County, Kansas (37.55, −100.85, web zoom 13.5), Kōnan-ku, Niigata (37.85, 139.05, z14), Pergamino, Buenos Aires (−33.72, −60.42, z13.5) and Rishton District, Fergana (40.45, 71.30, z14). Some Niigata paddies have no outline, and a few Kansas and Pergamino outlines are partial. The capture scripts are not in this repository.
- Geometry-tool figures (`assets/tools/`, October 29, 2026): `scripts/figures/tools_figs.py`. The per-tile chart plots the Python/GEOS reference and the one-thread Rust coverage simplify on the four largest tiles in global-ftw-2e [`rust/coverage-simplify/RESULTS.md`](https://github.com/taylor-geospatial/global-ftw-2e/blob/main/rust/coverage-simplify/RESULTS.md) (single observations on shared RAILS nodes, copied to `scripts/figures/data/coverage_simplify_tiles.csv`). The contourrs panels read the same Marshall County window as the model-input figure (published 2025 probability COG and Q1 mosaic), label four-connected pixels with p(field) ≥ 0.5 and p(boundary) < 0.5, drop regions under 64 pixels, and polygonize with contourrs 0.8.6; the script checks that rasterio returns the same WKB. This labelling illustrates polygonization and is not BoundaryVote. The per-tile polygonize times (19.9 s vs 28.4 s) and the two-tile parity check are from [ftw-global-data-catalog PR 53](https://github.com/fieldsoftheworld/ftw-global-data-catalog/pull/53); the 7.5× CDL figure is from the [contourrs performance notes](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/contourrs/performance/), which predate the current release; the coarsen numbers are from its [recorded coverage benchmark](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/coarsen/performance/).
